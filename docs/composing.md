# Composing with AlgoRhythms

Notes for the builder who rewrites the compositions after rev 9, and for any agent asked to write music with the instrument. Written by the session that made the three compositions (2026-11-20). The owner's verdict on that work: experimental pieces left them cold; songs with a melody, chords, a bass line and a form were loved.

## 2. Advice for composers

**Write music first, then translate.** Decide key, tempo, meter and form (intro, A, A′, B, A″, coda), the chord progression with explicit voicings, a singable melody, and a bass line, on a beat grid. Only then map them to moments. Every piece the owner liked was made this way; the "interesting" technical pieces (Shepard rise, overtone builds, 32-voice glissando fans) were not liked.

**Voicing and registers.**
- Give each part its own register and keep them apart: chords about 48–70, melody above (68–90), bass below 52, ornaments above the melody. With distinct notes, a melody landing on a pitch another line holds used to merge into it and die; parts fix that, but separate registers still sound clearer.
- Voice-lead chords by hand: keep common tones, move other voices by a step or two. Four chord voices are plenty.
- Glides are the instrument's character: chord changes of 0.7–1.4 s with a little stagger sound lush; melody portamento should be short (≈ 0.1 s) or it smears.

**Dynamics.**
- The engine normalises by 1/√(number of sounding voices), and silent voices still count. Adding voices does not make a piece louder; use `level` for the arc. Start quiet (the songs open around −24 dB and end around −18 dB, measured above 150 Hz).
- Voices start at level 1: set level moments at t = 0 with `over: 0` for anything meant to come in later, or the first instant blips.
- Measure loudness with the bass filtered out: an organ bass dominates plain RMS and hides the arc.
- Let voices breathe (owner, 2026-11-23: abrupt entries and exits "sound abrupt and bad"). A tune rest cuts a line in 20 ms and a comeback starts in 10 ms, so fade sustained voices with `level` moments: phrase ends over 0.15–0.25 s ending at the rest, entries over 40–80 ms. Struck timbres can start at once, but let them ring and fade the part rather than cutting the ring with a rest.

**Timing** (owner, 2026-11-23: a melody "a little out of sync").
- Set `stagger` and `jitter` on every glide. Moments inherit the piece's default glide (3 s, stagger 0.8, jitter 0.2 in `song.ts`); a one-line chord with `land: true` and no stagger moved up to 0.8 s before its beat.
- A landing glide starts moving `length` before the beat. Keep melody scoops at 30–50 ms, and give a lead a crisp, quick articulation (attack 15–20 ms, small `accent`); an 85 ms scoop with a soft bloom smeared every note against crisp accompaniment.
- No echo near a subdivision: a 115 ms delay at 116 bpm made every note sound late. Time delays to the beat or keep the voice dry.
- Swung melodies need something on the triplet grid underneath (a pulse with `step` = 1/3 beat), or they sound lazy over straight quarters.

**Timbres (built-ins).**
- `glass` is the safe pad and ornament.
- `reed` carries a melody well over glass.
- `organ` makes a good bass.
- `pluck` needs `pulse` with `strike`: its sustain is only 10 %.
- `sine` voices fuse into one tone (good for tricks, thin for music).
- `choir` is untested in a song.

**Walks.** A walk is an ornament, not the main event. It sounds musical in a pentatonic key (it can't clash with the chords), in a high register, with low density (0.3–0.5), a short `stepGlide` (0.05–0.06 s), on its own part. The owner's favourite moment in the old examples is the fast walk in Clockwork Heron ("Heron rises").

**Mechanics worth knowing.**
- A split's newborns fork from the nearest parent, so one split per added voice makes a nice "unfurling" entrance (split and merge ignored stagger before rev 9).
- `shift` by 0 scale steps snaps every line into the key in effect.
- At a modulation, pin or tune the bass to the new root, or the change is vague.
- Prefer region moments (`walk`, `pulse`, `tune`) to hundreds of point moments: the lane, share links and tests all suffer from big moment counts.

**Checking without ears.**
- Plot the compiled lines (`pitchAt` sampled over time) to see the shape.
- Check loudness per section and look for clicks (outliers in the second difference).
- Check that no line dies unexpectedly (count active voices over time).
- Then give the owner a render: they are the judge.

**The experimental direction (Night Sleeper, 2026-11-23).** The owner liked a chord-move "dream" inside a song more than the song around it, and asked for that flavour throughout. What made it work:
- Harmony made of moves, each with its own glide length (blooms 3–4 s staggered from the centre, planes 3 s with no stagger, a 5 s yawn), all landing on downbeats.
- One crossing, all lines through one point: a mirror with stagger 0, jitter 0 and one length. A lydian maj7♯11 voicing mirrored about its middle line (the major seventh) lands on the lydian chord a major third up, so a fold can also modulate.
- Something steady under the moves: a pulse (rail joints), an arpeggio that plays whatever chord is there (a music box), a bell on arrivals.
- Lydian chords hold a semitone under the root and the fifth (the major seventh, the ♯11), so tunes, bells and doublings avoid singing those against them.

**What works well**
- Numerals with `close` voicing in a register give good voice leading for free, and a `key` moment carries them.
- Fourths (D G C F A) planed by `shift` stay clash-free on i, ii and v of dorian.
- Births are entrances: a part brought in by a chord with `count` swoops out of the nearest line. (Correction, 2026-11-22: only within its part; a silent part's newborns have no line of their own part to fork from, so they fade in on their notes.)
- One voice per part for microtonal work; `snap: 'none'` spreads, then `shift 0 scaleSteps` to resolve.

**Traps met**
- Only the first establishing moment starts from silence. A `tune` or `unison` on a silent part does nothing (an `info` diagnostic only). Bring a part in with a `chord` + `count`. (Fixed 2026-11-22, suggestion #4: a chord, unison or tune on a silent part brings it in; what still does nothing there is a warning.)
- A one-line part given a chord symbol takes whichever chord tone the voicing picks (often the 3rd or 5th), not the root. Basses are tunes. (Fixed 2026-11-22, suggestion #2: one line now takes the root or slash bass.)
- Tunes have no rests, and a repeated pitch is held, not re-struck.
- Roman numerals count major-scale degrees in every mode: in minor write `bIII bVI bVII`; `VI` in A minor is F♯.
- A `shift` past a line's range folds it an octave, onto another line of its part, which then merges and is lost. Give planed parts an octave of headroom. (Fixed 2026-11-22, suggestion #6: the fold takes the next octave or the nearest free scale step instead, with a `fold-apart` warning. The headroom advice still keeps the shape.)
- Walks follow the key, not the chord. Over a pentatonic walk, use pad chords made only of the scale's notes (Dm7, F6, Csus2, Gsus4), or set the key to the pentatonic (Porch Light does, for its bells; numerals don't care).
- A part joining lowers every other line (the 1/√n normalisation): about −1.8 dB when 3 bells join 6 lines. Compensate with `level`.
- Pulses with `floor: 0` draw as specks (lines show their level); a floor of 0.1–0.2 keeps the rope visible.
- `nearest` voicing on a 3-voice pad packs maj7 chords into semitone clusters (A–B♭ at A3).

**Checking** (scratch tools, not committed): a semitone-clash finder over audible lines (m2/M7/m9 held ≥ 0.3 s), loudness per 8 s window and per part, headless screenshots via the `__algorhythms` hook. Renders: `renders/compositions-2/*.m4a`.

**Suggestions from this set** (owner: "I like your suggestions", 2026-11-21; not yet scheduled):
1. A chord-progression moment ("IV V iii vi" typed once, like a tune): half of Porch Light's moments are single chord changes.
2. Root in the bass: a chord symbol on a one-line part should be able to take the root.
3. Tunes: rests, re-struck repeated notes, and transposing with a key change.
4. A part can start from silence partway through (tune, unison or chord on a silent part), and the no-op today should be a warning, not `info`.
5. Walks over changing harmony: a "chord tones" option, or a walk scale separate from the key.
6. Range folding must not merge a line into another of its part (it is lost for the rest of the piece).
7. Pulsed lines drawn by their envelope, with marks for hits, so floor-0 pulses don't draw as dust.
8. Targets that name several parts ("all but Bells").
9. An arpeggio note order for pulses (1 3 2 4), so arpeggios line up with the bar.
10. Roman numerals keep the major-scale ruler (owner, 2026-11-21, after weighing both conventions): it means the same thing in every mode and works for pentatonic keys (Porch Light relies on it). Add an inspector hint instead: when a typed numeral falls outside a minor-type key's scale, suggest the flat ("`VI` here is F♯; did you mean `♭VI` = F?").
11. Smaller: compensate the 1/√n drop when a part joins; the HUD chord name in tune-driven pieces.
