# Composing with AlgoRhythms

Notes for the builder who rewrites the compositions after rev 9, and for any agent asked to write music with the instrument. Written by the session that made the three compositions (2026-11-20). The owner's verdict on that work: experimental pieces left them cold; songs with a melody, chords, a bass line and a form were loved.

## 2. Advice for composers

**Write music first, then translate.** Decide key, tempo, meter and form (intro, A, A′, B, A″, coda), the chord progression with explicit voicings, a singable melody, and a bass line, on a beat grid. Only then map them to moments. Every piece the owner liked was made this way; the "interesting" technical pieces (Shepard rise, overtone builds, 32-voice glissando fans) were not liked.

**Voicing and registers.**
- Give each part its own register and keep them apart: chords about 48–70, melody above (68–90), bass below 52, ornaments above the melody. With distinct notes, a melody landing on a pitch another line holds used to merge into it and die; parts fix that, but separate registers still sound clearer.
- Voice-lead chords by hand: keep common tones, move other voices by a step or two. Four chord voices are plenty.
- Glides are the instrument's character: chord changes of 0.7–1.4 s with a little stagger sound lush; melody portamento should be short (≈ 0.1 s) or it smears.

**Dynamics.**
- The engine normalises by 1/√(number of sounding voices), and silent voices still count. Adding voices does not make a piece louder; use `level` for the arc. Start quiet (the songs open around −24 dB and end around −18 dB, measured above 150 Hz).
- Voices start at level 1: set level moments at t = 0 with `over: 0` for anything meant to come in later, or the first instant blips.
- Measure loudness with the bass filtered out: an organ bass dominates plain RMS and hides the arc.

**Timbres (built-ins).**
- `glass` is the safe pad and ornament.
- `reed` carries a melody well over glass.
- `organ` makes a good bass.
- `pluck` needs `pulse` with `strike`: its sustain is only 10 %.
- `sine` voices fuse into one tone (good for tricks, thin for music).
- `choir` is untested in a song.

**Walks.** A walk is an ornament, not the main event. It sounds musical in a pentatonic key (it can't clash with the chords), in a high register, with low density (0.3–0.5), a short `stepGlide` (0.05–0.06 s), on its own part. The owner's favourite moment in the old examples is the fast walk in Clockwork Heron ("Heron rises").

**Mechanics worth knowing.**
- A split's newborns fork from the nearest parent, so one split per added voice makes a nice "unfurling" entrance (split and merge ignored stagger before rev 9).
- `shift` by 0 scale steps snaps every line into the key in effect.
- At a modulation, pin or tune the bass to the new root, or the change is vague.
- Prefer region moments (`walk`, `pulse`, `tune`) to hundreds of point moments: the lane, share links and tests all suffer from big moment counts.

**Checking without ears.**
- Plot the compiled lines (`pitchAt` sampled over time) to see the shape.
- Check loudness per section and look for clicks (outliers in the second difference).
- Check that no line dies unexpectedly (count active voices over time).
- Then give the owner a render: they are the judge.
